# Blueprint — Srinivasa Ramanujan

## One Sentence
The mathematician who worked backward — arriving at the answer through intuition, then leaving the proof for others (sometimes decades later) to catch up.

## Hero Metaphor — 3 candidates

1. **The Inverted Proof** *(recommended)* — a two-track diagram. Top rail: the conventional path (Axiom → Proof → Theorem). Bottom rail: his path (Theorem arrives → Proof follows — one node even marked "proved 2012," referencing the mock theta functions verified decades after his death). Directly teaches the mental model instead of just decorating it, and gives a clean structural contrast to Newton's single-line chain.
2. **The Notebook Cascade** — a dense vertical waterfall of numbered theorem-nodes, mostly unproven, a handful bridged by sparse "proof" connectors. Visualizes sheer density and the answer-first mode; more atmospheric, teaches less on its own.
3. **The Signal Without the Static** — incoming wave/transmission converging into crisp theorem nodes, with a "Hardy" filter downstream verifying them. Evocative, but risks tipping into a mystical-genius trope rather than depicting disciplined intuition — he did credit his insights to the goddess Namagiri, so this needs care if used at all.

**Recommendation: #1, The Inverted Proof.** Most accurate to how mathematicians actually describe his process, and the two-rail structure is a genuinely new diagram shape for the site (nobody else is a side-by-side comparison).

## Thinking Process
Self-taught almost entirely from one book — Carr's *Synopsis of Elementary Results in Pure and Applied Mathematics*, a terse compendium of ~6,000 theorems with minimal proof, which shaped his own habit of stating results without derivation. Filled ordinary notebooks (calculations done on a slate first, paper being too expensive to waste on drafts) with thousands of original results — many correct, some famously so exotic that Hardy called them "scarcely possible to believe." He didn't distrust proof; he simply treated it as a separate, later task from discovery. Hardy's own verdict: results "defeated me completely; I had never seen anything in the least like them before... they must be true, because if they were not true, no one would have had the imagination to invent them."

## Mental Models (borrowable)
1. **Pattern-first, proof-later** — separate the act of generating a hypothesis from the act of verifying it; don't let rigor gate discovery.
2. **Constraint as compression** — writing on a slate before committing to paper forced him to hold and refine ideas mentally before "spending" the page.
3. **Pair intuition with a rigorous foil** — his partnership with Hardy paired a generator with a verifier; neither role alone gets you all the way.
4. **The notebook as compounding capital** — years of raw, unordered results became a personal asset later generations are still mining.
5. **Depth over breadth** — total absorption in one domain (he neglected every other college subject to the point of failing) produced results a well-rounded curriculum never would have.

## Big Ideas
- Highly composite numbers and the partition function's asymptotic behavior
- The Ramanujan–Hardy "taxicab number" (1729) and the idea that even a mundane number hides structure if you look
- Mock theta functions — introduced in his final "Lost Notebook" (1920), not understood until decades later, now used in string theory and black hole entropy calculations
- Infinite series and continued fractions so unconventional Hardy initially assumed some must be wrong

## Timeline
- **1887** — Born in Erode, Tamil Nadu
- **1903** — Given Carr's *Synopsis*, ~6,000 theorems with minimal proof — the book that shaped his style
- **1904–1906** — Fails college exams, having neglected every subject but mathematics
- **1909** — Marries Janaki
- **1911–12** — First paper published in the *Journal of the Indian Mathematical Society*
- **1913** — Writes to G. H. Hardy at Cambridge with pages of unproven theorems
- **1914** — Sails to England; collaboration with Hardy begins
- **1916** — Earns a Cambridge degree by research
- **1917** — Elected Fellow of the Royal Society — one of the youngest ever, first Indian
- **1918** — First Indian elected Fellow of Trinity College, Cambridge
- **1919** — Returns to India, health failing
- **1920** — Dies at 32 in Kumbakonam, leaving the "Lost Notebook"
- **2012–present** — Mock theta functions fully proven and extended; found applications in physics

## One Last Lesson
The answer can arrive before the proof — but you still need someone in your life who demands the proof anyway.
