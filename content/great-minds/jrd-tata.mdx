In 1953, the Indian government nationalized Air India, taking the airline out of the Tata family's hands. Its very next move was to name J. R. D. Tata the airline's first chairman anyway. He held that chairmanship for twenty-five more years — longer than most executives hold any job.

It ended in 1978, when Prime Minister Morarji Desai removed him. Not a market downturn. Not a boardroom vote. One person's decision.

That gap — between who legally owns something and who is actually trusted to run it — is the real subject of this page. JRD Tata spent fifty-three years as Chairman of Tata Sons proving that the second kind of authority is sturdier than the first. He built it the same way three times over: with his airline, with the businesses inside the Tata Group, and with the people he chose to run them.
