In 1592, the first thing anyone wrote down about William Shakespeare as a playwright was an insult. A rival named Robert Greene, dying and bitter, published a pamphlet warning fellow university-educated writers about an actor who'd started writing plays of his own — an "upstart crow, beautified with our feathers," Greene called him, implying he'd stolen his craft from better men. Shakespeare had no university degree. He'd arrived in London from Stratford-upon-Avon sometime in the previous seven years — a stretch of his life so undocumented that historians simply call it "the lost years" — and by every mark of pedigree that mattered to Greene's circle, he didn't belong.

Two years later, Shakespeare made a decision most playwrights of his era never got the chance to make. Instead of continuing to sell individual scripts for a flat fee — the standard arrangement, which ended a writer's stake in a play the moment it was paid for — he became a founding shareholder of the Lord Chamberlain's Men. He now owned a fraction of everything the company earned, indefinitely, not a one-time payment for each new play. Five years after that, he and his fellow shareholders personally financed an entire theater, the Globe, so the company also controlled the building its plays were performed in. Playwright, actor, and landlord all sat on the same side of the ledger.

That arrangement is easy to skip past in favor of the plays themselves, but it explains something the plays alone can't: how, seven years after Shakespeare's death, roughly half of them still existed to be collected into a single published edition at all. Two of his fellow shareholders, John Heminges and Henry Condell, took on the work of compiling the First Folio in 1623 — not out of institutional duty, but because they were his business partners, and the plays were, in a real financial sense, partly theirs too.

The stake he insisted on owning — in the company, in the theater, in the outcome — is the thread running through everything else on this page, including how much of his work survived him at all.
