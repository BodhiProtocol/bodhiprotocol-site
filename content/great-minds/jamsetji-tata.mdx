The Taj Mahal Palace opened its doors in Bombay in December 1903. Jamsetji Tata was in the room. It's the only one of his four founding visions he ever was.

He died five months later, in a hotel room in Germany, having already pledged half his fortune to a research institute that wouldn't admit a student for another five years, and having already written his son a letter about a steel town's street widths for a mill that wouldn't exist for another three. A hydroelectric plant he wanted running Bombay's textile mills wouldn't send power anywhere for another eleven.

None of that stopped him from deciding. That's the real subject of this page — not the four institutions themselves, all of which still exist, but the arithmetic behind starting three of them anyway, on a timeline he could do the math on well enough to know he wouldn't be there for the ending.
